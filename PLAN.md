# Messy Room: App Plan

A web app that helps kids organize and clean their rooms. It works entirely with pictures, so children who can't read yet can use it on their own. Words are always optional, for readers or a helping grown-up.

---

## 0. Who are you? (the very first screen)

When the app opens, it always asks **"Who are you?"** and shows everyone's sticker as a big circle, plus a **➕ New** circle.

- **Each person has a profile:** an optional name, plus one sticker picked from the shared sticker collection
- **✏️ pencil** on each person opens their sticker page
- **Any number of people:** 1 kid, 8 kids, or a parent doing their own room
- **Tap your sticker** to go to the home screen. Your sticker shows in the corner, and tapping it switches person.
- **Rooms and rewards are shared** by everyone. Profiles are how jobs get assigned to a person later (like "vacuum the living room").

## 0b. Home screen

A row of **big, round circles**, each with a picture sticker so it's easy to see and tap. No reading needed.

| Circle | What happens when you tap it | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Rooms** | Opens a list of circles, one for each room: **My Room** first. Later: Bathroom, Living Room, Kitchen and more. | My Room now, other rooms later |
| **Stickers** | Opens your sticker page (same as the ✏️ pencil). See section 2. | Now |
| **Rewards** | Opens a page where kids collect the rewards their parents promised them, like money, a game, or a special date or outing. | Now |
| **Clean** | A parent or child lists jobs the parent asked for, like "pick up my stuff from the living room," "vacuum the living room" or "wipe the kitchen counters." Each job shows a picture of the room plus a picture of the tool (rag, spray bottle, sponge, vacuum). Choosing a job highlights that part of the room, like the kitchen counter. | Later |

The circles appear in this order: **Rooms, Stickers, Rewards, Clean.**

All buttons that lead to more choices are also round circles with pictures, so every screen feels the same.

**Circles that aren't built yet** (Bathroom, Living Room, Kitchen, Clean) show **grayed out** as "coming soon."

### Rewards

- **Rewards are listed as circles**, the same way rooms are
- **Each reward has a picture:** take a photo (candy, a toy, a trip to a favorite restaurant) or choose a picture already on the device
- **Parents can add a reward, change its picture or delete it** (behind the PIN)
- **The parent sets a goal** for each reward, like "5 cleanups = a game"
- **A cleanup counts automatically** when the child puts everything away in a room or finishes a deep clean. Each one counts toward the reward right away.
- **When the goal is reached**, the reward is ready to collect on the Rewards page
- **Parent "Earned" button:** a parent can also mark a reward as earned whenever they choose. This button is **behind a PIN**, so kids can't tap it themselves.

### Parent PIN

One PIN guards everything parent-only:

- Marking a reward as earned
- Adding rewards, changing their pictures and goals, and deleting them
- Changing how often cleaning jobs come up
- Deleting a room
- Removing a furniture part that already has things assigned to it
- Deleting a person
- Deleting a sticker (only ones nobody is using)

## 1. Who it's for and where it runs

- **Kids**, including those who can't read yet
- **Parents or helpers**, who can add labels, change settings and help with setup
- **Devices:**
  - **Tablet and computer:** full picture version, made for kids
  - **Phone:** opens in the word list version for parents, with the picture version one tap away
- **Shared rooms:** works for 1 to 4 children in the same room

## 2. Stickers ("Who am I?")

### Shared sticker collection
- **All stickers are shared.** Everyone sees the whole collection on the sticker page.
- **One sticker per person:** a sticker someone is using is **taken**. It shows grayed out with that person's name and can't be picked or changed by anyone else, so siblings don't fight over it.
- **No two stickers can look exactly the same.** If you try, the app says it's already made.
- **Your sticker has a ✔️.** Tap any free sticker to switch to it, ✏️ to change a free one, or ➕ to make a new one (a new one becomes yours).
- **Starter stickers:** the collection starts with 26 ready-made stickers (at least one of every animal, plus dragons, aliens and people). Kids pick one and change it however they like.
- **Only parents can delete stickers** (with the PIN), and only stickers nobody is using
- **Deleting a person** also needs the PIN. Their sticker goes back into the collection.

### Making a sticker
- **Start from any ready-made sticker:** the sticker maker opens with a "Start from" row of all the ready-made stickers (every animal, dragons, aliens, people). Tap one, then change anything.
- **Changing a sticker makes a new copy:** ✏️ never replaces the sticker you started from; it stays in the collection. The new copy becomes the sticker of the person whose page it was made on.
- **Style:** floating faces (no bodies), flat shapes, a white sticker outline, and simple line eyes and mouths
- **Build the expression** (every sticker), piece by piece:
  - **Eyes:** open, shiny, big and sparkly (white around a big pupil), almond, almond with a cat-like slit, wide, closed happy, sleepy, wink, looking up, down or to the side, cross-eyed, crying, hearts, angry, sad
  - **Mouth:** closed smile, open smile, smile with teeth, small smile, tongue out, "oh," frown, wobbly, flat
  - **Cheeks:** none, blush, shy lines
  - **Extras:** sweat drop, Zzz, question marks, heart, sparkle, anger mark
  - **Quick feelings** (happy, laugh, cute, silly, love, shy, sleepy, surprised, nervous, confused, sad, grumpy) are only starting points: one tap fills in the parts, and any part can then be changed
- **Person:** expression, face shape (oval, round, long, square, heart), skin color, hair style (short, long, curly, long curly, two braids, bun, pigtails, spiky, bald), hair color, hat, glasses, background
- **Hats are worn, not perched:** a beanie or nightcap sits down over the hair and hides the hair on top; only hair that hangs down (pigtails, braids, long hair, curls) shows.
- **More colors in the sticker maker:** every color row (skin, hair, fur, background) has the ➕ color picker
- **Alien:** expression, color (including gray and dark space green), head shape (round, tall, wide, extra wide, bulb, classic), ears (none, pointy, round), skin (plain, spots, stars), 1 to 3 eyes, antennae (none, 1 or 2; straight, spiral, zigzag or crooked), hat, glasses, background
- **Animal:** cat, dog, chihuahua, bunny, bear, mouse, fox, sheep or baby dragon (each with its own face, not a cat face with new ears), then expression, coat (plain, tabby, patch, two-tone, calico, colorpoint), fur color, patch color, hat, glasses and background. Calico cat is the starting animal.
- **Cats also get:** face shape (round, wide, fluffy), ears (pointy, big, folded; folded isn't offered on the wide face) and long whiskers
- **Baby dragon:** face shape round (default) or chubby; bat wings on the top corners of the head (a thick arm bone rises to an elbow, runs out to the tip, and thin ribs fan down to a scalloped edge); cream freckles, little brows; hair tuft is a choice (none, sprout, single, curl, spikes, puff); horns are optional and tiny
- **Hats:** party hat, tiny crown, tiny bowler hat, beanie, cap, bow (on the ear), flower, top hat, nightcap (the beanie pulled down over the eyes was taken out)
- **Bow tie** under the chin (pink or blue)
- **Glasses:** round, sunglasses, hearts, stars
- **Face shapes vary:** round faces are fine, but each kind offers other shapes too, so stickers don't all look alike
- **Keep it really simple:** a few big choices per step, each shown as a small picture
- **"This is me" on the map:** the child can drag their sticker to where they're sitting (like on the bed) and play with it

**Whose space is whose:**

- Each space (drawer, cubby, shelf) gets a small badge with its owner's sticker
- **Setting owners (decided): whole piece first, then any part.** Open a piece and tap someone's sticker to make the whole piece theirs. Then open any single drawer, cubby, pocket or hook to give it to someone else, or tap more than one sticker to share it.
- **Who can change owners (decided):** anyone can claim an empty space or add themselves to share one. Taking a space away from someone else needs the parent PIN.
- **Shared spaces** show more than one badge, like a book shelf for two kids
- **During cleanup**, the child taps their own sticker first. The app shows their spaces and the shared ones, and dims everyone else's.

## 3. Building the room

Everything is drag and drop. No measuring.

### How the room is shown (decided)
- **Front view is for building pieces; the map from above is for placing them.**
- **Step A, pick your furniture first (front view):** choose a piece and shape it to match the real one
  - **Dressers:** how many drawers; tall and skinny or short and wide. Drawers are plain spaces with no inserts.
  - **Cubby units:** how many cubbies; each cubby is open, has a bin, a paper sorter or a paper drawer box
  - **Cupboards:** come with a middle shelf, so each door opens onto a top spot and a bottom spot
  - **Wall shelves:** short or long
  - **Closet:** shown with no doors, so you see the inside: how many rods and shelves, where things hang and where things sit
    - **Sections:** a closet is split into sections side by side, with a divider between them. Each section has its own width and is one high rod, one low rod a child can reach, two rods (top and bottom), or a stack of shelves. A narrower section gives a shorter rod.
    - **Top shelf** goes all the way across, over every section (0 to 3 of them)
    - **Ready-made closets:** single (one rod), single split in half (one rod, and two rods on the other half), a rod on each side with shelves in the middle, and double wide with a divider (one rod on the left; two rods on the right, one for shirts and one for pants)
    - **Closet doors:** folding (bifold), a fabric curtain, sliding, one regular door, or no door. Each looks different on the map from above, so a closet reads as a closet.
    - **Inside and outside (decided):** the closet opens showing its inside. A big 🚪 button switches to the outside of the doors, where things hang (like a pocket organizer), and back. Kids never have to aim between "open the door" and "open the organizer."
    - **Furniture can go inside the closet:** the closet has a floor where you place pieces you've already built (like a small dresser or a cubby unit), and they open the same way they do in the room
- **Step B, the map from above** (like looking down from the ceiling): place the door, the closet and the windows where they really are, rotate the room to match, and drag each piece's top to its spot. Each top matches the piece's shape, so a skinny dresser has a narrow top.
- **Tapping a piece on the map opens its front view.** Each drawer, cubby, cupboard spot, shelf or rod can be opened to see what's kept there.

### The map, in order (decided)
1. **📐 Room size first:** set how wide and how deep the room is, by eye, by dragging its edges (or ➖ ➕). It can still be stretched later.
2. **🚪 Walls next:** the door, the closet and the windows (and the light switch) go on the walls before any furniture
3. **🛏️ Furniture last:** every piece already built waits beside the room, ready to drag in. A piece dropped near a wall turns so its back is against the wall.
- **Turn the room to match what you see:** a big **rotate button** turns the map a quarter turn each tap, at any step, until it matches what the child sees from where they're sitting

### Furniture and wall pieces
Ready-made pieces to start from (not every possible option, just good ones). Any of them can be changed with "change the parts."

- **Beds:** twin, bunk bed, queen, king, and a loft bed with a couch underneath that folds down into a bed. Bed frames count as something to clean later.
- **Dressers:** 4 drawers; wide with 4 or 5 wide drawers; tall and skinny with 6; short and wide with 2 × 3; a short one with 2 drawers over 2 cupboard doors; and a long one with 3 drawers on each side and a cupboard in the middle (one door, with a middle shelf inside making a top spot and a bottom spot; no open cubby)
- **Side tables:** 1 drawer (with an open space under it) or 2 drawers
- **Drawer containers:** the small plastic kind with 3, 4 or 5 drawers, with or without wheels
- **Cubby units:** 2 × 4 tall, 3 × 4 (12) and 4 × 4, sized like real ones (a 4 × 4 is almost 5 feet each way). Rows and columns can be added or removed. Each cubby is one of:
  - **open** (books fit here)
  - **a bin** (any color, including white)
  - **a paper sorter** with little trays (5 to start, 2 to 8), each tray its own space
  - **a paper drawer box** (3 drawers to start, 1 to 5, dark or white or any color), each drawer its own space
- **Bookshelves**
- **Seats:** chair, beanbag, floor chair, lounge chair with a footrest that kicks up
- **Wire shelves:** medium height, 5 shelves
- **Over-the-door pocket organizer:** choose how many pockets across and down (like 4 × 6). Each pocket is its own space and can hold anything: shoes, medicine, creams, beauty products. Hangs on any door (room door, bathroom door, closet door).
- **Hooks:** single, double, a set of three over a door, a belt rack, and wall hooks for things like aprons and backpacks. Each hook is its own space.
- **Wall file sorter** with key hooks
- **Laundry hampers** (round plastic and rectangular), **baskets, bags and boxes**
- **Trash cans:** kitchen (tall with a lid and foot pedal), small rectangle, round small to medium, and a blue recycling bin with the recycling arrows. Useful later for jobs like taking out the trash and the recycling.
- **The top of a piece is a space too**, like a TV and a dish sitting on top of the cubbies
- **Closet** (inside view, see above)
- **On the walls:** windows, shelves, light switch (drawn as a switch plate with a light bulb, so it isn't mistaken for an outlet), outlets (useful later for jobs like plugging in the vacuum), hooks, wall file sorter
- **Furniture is 3-D on the map:** each piece's top shows its real width and depth. A tall skinny dresser has a narrow top, but it's still a full drawer deep. Beds are their real size next to everything else (twin, bunk and loft are long and narrow; queen and king are wider).
- **Stretch on the map:** tap a door, window, closet or piece of furniture and drag its yellow handle (or use ➖ ➕) to make it wider. Furniture can also be made deeper or shallower. A double-wide closet can reach halfway across a wall; a cubby unit can fill a third of one.
- **Closets are built into a wall (decided):** on the map a closet sits on the other side of the wall, outside the room. The wall has an opening where its doors are (the folding-door zigzag, curtain line and so on). Drag it along any wall like a window, and stretch it wider or deeper.
- **Closets are built first, then put on a wall (decided):** a closet is not a door and has its own picture. In the 🚪 step the tray holds the door, window, light switch, outlet and a **➕ new closet** (a closet front with folding doors). Tapping it opens the closet builder: pick single, split, shelves in the middle or double wide, then the door style, rods, shelves and what stands on its floor, and ✔️ Save. The finished closet then waits in the tray as its own picture, ready to drag onto its wall and stretch to fit. Closets are not in the furniture ➕ list.
- **Room size by eye:** the room grows on its own to make space, and you can drag an edge to make it wider or longer
- **Room shape:** rectangle for now (odd shapes like L-shaped rooms could come later)

### Who builds it (decided)
- **Both the child and a grown-up, depending on the step**
- **Ready-made pieces** (like "6-drawer dresser" or "12-cubby unit") are shown as pictures the child can drag in alone
- **A big ✔️ Save button** on every piece, at the top and at the bottom, so it's clear changes are kept
- **Size is always showing** (↔️ wider/narrower, ↕️ taller/shorter) right under the picture, not hidden behind 🔧
- **Every piece can change size:** beds, chairs, hampers and trash cans too, on their page and on the map (wider with the yellow handle or ↔️, deeper or longer with ⤵️). Hooks and pocket organizers get bigger or smaller as a whole (🔍).
- **Tap a cubby to change what's in it:** take the basket out (open), or put in a bin, a paper sorter or paper drawers
- **Drag baskets:** drag a bin, sorter or paper-drawer box from one cubby to another (if the other cubby is full, they swap, and what's kept inside moves with the basket). A row under the picture holds a bin, a sorter and paper drawers to drag into any cubby, and a 🗑️ circle to drop one on to take it out.
- **A version number** shows small on the home screen, so it's easy to tell which copy of the app is open
- **"Change the parts"** is a simple option a grown-up can open when a ready-made piece doesn't match the real one
- **Anyone can add parts** or change an empty part
- **Removing a part that already has things assigned to it needs the parent PIN**, so a child can't lose what they set up by accident

### Furniture is built from parts
Real furniture comes in all shapes and sizes, so each piece is made of parts you choose:

- **Drawers**, **cubbies**, **cupboard doors**, **open shelves**
- You choose how many and how they're arranged. Examples:
  - 12-cubby unit
  - Skinny dresser with 6 drawers
  - Side table with 2 drawers, side table with 1 drawer
  - Dresser with 2 small drawers and 2 cupboard doors
- **Every part opens on its own.** Tapping a drawer opens a window showing what's kept inside, like shirts or underwear.

### Colors and labels
- **Change any piece's color** (the blue dresser, the pink shelf), so kids can tell things apart without reading
- **More colors:** a ➕ circle after the color choices opens a big pop-up grid of color squares (each color family from light to dark, plus browns and grays). No sliders. A color picked there shows up in the row.
- **Optional labels** for readers or helpers

### Step 4: Decide what goes where
- ✅ Built: open any drawer, cubby, cupboard space, pocket, hook, shelf, rod or hamper and pick pictures under **🧺 What goes here?** (more than one is fine, like socks and underwear). They show small in that space on the furniture. **📷** takes a photo of anything not on the list.
- **The pictures:** clothes (shirts, pants, shorts, dresses, pajamas, socks, underwear, jackets, coats, scarves, shoes, hats, belts); play (toys, stuffed animals, games and puzzles, books, art supplies, music, electronics); everyday (school papers, backpacks, aprons, keys, purse, phone, tablet, charger, medicine, lotion, creams, make-up, hair things, candle); other (blankets, a mix of things). These become the item stickers later.
- Each space is given what it holds: the closet holds clothes, one shelf holds toys, another holds books or a mix
- **The child decides.** There's no wrong answer, as long as they know where things go.

## 4. Item stickers (the things that get put away)

Every item is a sticker the child can drag.

- **Item stickers start from the same pictures as "what goes here" (decided).** Emoji can't be recolored exactly, so the things kids recolor are drawn in the same shapes: shirt, pants, shorts, dress, pajamas, socks, underwear, jacket, coat, scarf, hat, shoes, stuffed animal, purse, backpack, blanket. Any color (including the ➕ picker) and a pattern: stripes, dots, flowers, hearts or stars. Things nobody recolors (phone, keys, medicine, candle) stay as emoji.

- **Some items take more than one space:** a pair of shoes takes two pockets side by side in a pocket organizer

- **🧸 Things (decided):** a circle on the home screen. Things belong to the whole house (a backpack moves from the bedroom to the kitchen), not to one room, and tidying starts from them.
- ✅ Built: ➕ make a thing: pick its picture (or 📷 a photo), color (including ➕ more colors) and pattern, 👤 whose it is, and 📍 where it lives (pick a piece of furniture, then tap the drawer, cubby, shelf or hook). An optional label. The Things page shows each thing with its owner's sticker and a 📍 when it has a home.
- **Two ways to make an item:**
  - **Take a photo** of the real thing, like a favorite stuffed animal
  - **Pick a cartoon drawing** (dress, shirt, pants, socks, teddy bear, puzzle, game, book, musical instrument, trash and more)
- **Pop-up color chooser** to make a drawing any color (a pink dress)
- **Patterns** like flowers, stripes or dots (a flowered shirt)
- Item stickers can be saved, reused and deleted

## 5. Tidying up (one thing at a time)

**Decided:**
- **Where it starts:** the Clean circle opens 🧺 Tidy up (now) and 🧽 Jobs (stage 5)
- **Show the spot:** the app shows where each thing goes, with the spot glowing 📍. The child puts the real thing away, then drags its picture onto the glowing spot (or taps ✔️). A "let them find it" game could be a parent setting later.
- **Two ways to go:** one thing at a time, or one kind at a time (all the trash, then all the dishes, then all the clothes)
- **What's on the floor:** your things, kinds of things (socks, books, toys), plus:
  - 🗑️ **Trash** → the trash cans: tissues, wrappers, food scraps, juice boxes, snack bags, scrap paper
  - ♻️ **Recycling** → the recycling bin: cans, boxes and cardboard, paper, plastic bottles
  - 🍽️ **Dishes** → "take to the kitchen": cups, mugs, plates, bowls, spoons and forks, water bottles
  - 🧺 **Dirty clothes** → the hampers
- Only the child's own spaces and shared ones glow; everyone else's are dimmed
- **Done:** confetti, and the cleanup counts toward Rewards

1. **Who's cleaning?** The child taps their sticker.
2. **What's on the floor?** The child picks from a checklist: trash, clothes, toys, puzzles, games, musical instruments and so on.
   - **Pictures / Words tabs** switch between picture cards and a word list
3. **One group at a time:** the app shows one group (like toys) and the room map
4. **Put it away:** the child drags each item sticker to where it belongs. Tapping a drawer or cubby opens it.
5. **Celebrate:** sound and confetti when a group is done 🎉
6. **Next group**, until the floor is clear

## 6. Deep clean (after tidying)

- **Which jobs show up (decided):** the app suggests jobs from what's in the room (a bed means wipe the bed frame, a light switch means disinfect it), and a parent can turn any job on or off (PIN).
- **Who does each job (decided):** jobs follow ownership. A job on Ava's dresser is Ava's. Jobs for shared things or the whole room (vacuum the floor, disinfect the doorknob) belong to everyone who shares the room, and whoever does it first checks it off. A parent can give any job to a specific person instead.

A picture checklist of cleaning jobs. The app suggests them based on what's in the room.

| Job | Comes up because the room has… |
|---|---|
| Vacuum the floor | Every room |
| Take dirty clothes to the laundry room | Clothes |
| Dust the dresser, shelves, side tables | Those pieces |
| Wipe down, dust or vacuum the bed frame | A bed |
| Disinfect the light switch | A light switch |
| Disinfect the doorknob | The door |

### How often
- **Every day (default):** tidy the floor, dirty clothes to the laundry
- **Once a week (default):** dust, vacuum, wipe the bed frame
- **Parent settings:** change any job to daily, weekly, or "only when I choose"
- Sound and confetti when jobs are done 🎉

## 7. Saving

- **Saves automatically** on the device, so the room is there every day
- **Backup and restore** ✅ built: a 🔒 ⚙️ button on the home screen (parent PIN) opens the backup page
  - **Copy backup** copies everything (people, stickers, rewards with photos, rooms) as one block of text to paste into a note or an email. Inside claude.ai downloads are blocked, so copying is the main way.
  - **Save as a file** works when the app is opened on its own website
  - **Restore** from pasted text or a chosen file, on this device or another one. It asks before replacing everything.
  - Shows when the last backup was made
- No accounts or sign-in needed

## 8. Look and feel

- Big, friendly buttons that are easy to tap
- Every circle's background color is different from its picture, so pictures never fade into the circle (for example, a yellow gift sits on purple)
- Cartoon style for furniture, items and stickers
- Very few words on the kid screens. Pictures do the explaining.
- Picture version and word list version can be switched anytime

## 9. Building it in stages

Each stage gives something usable before moving on.

0. **Home screen** ✅ built: "Who are you?" profiles, Rooms, Stickers, Rewards, Clean (grayed out), sticker builder, shared rewards with parent PIN
1. **Room builder** ✅ built: door, rotate, beds, furniture from parts, windows, light switch, colors, labels, stretching the room
   - ✅ Step A built: pick ready-made pieces, shape them with 🔧 "change the parts," colors, optional labels, tap any part to open it, closet with rods, shelves and furniture on its floor
   - ✅ Closets built first (➕ in the walls tray), closet doors and the 🚪 inside/outside button, outlets, a door page for hanging things on a door, pocket organizers, hooks, wall file sorter, wire shelves, lounge chair, hampers, baskets, bags and boxes, the top of each piece as a space, and a pop-up color picker
   - ✅ Step B built: the map from above in three steps (📐 room size, 🚪 door, closet, windows and light switch, 🛏️ furniture), big rotate button, dragging from the tray, pieces turning their backs to the wall, tap a piece to turn it, open it or take it off the map
2. **People stickers and ownership** ✅ built: sticker builder, a 👤 Whose? row for each piece and each space, owner badges on spaces, shared spaces, the PIN to take a space away from someone, and 🧺 what goes where
3. **Item stickers** ✅ built: 🧸 Things with the same pictures (recolorable drawings), colors, patterns, photos, owners and a home spot
4. **Tidying** ✅ built: Clean → 🧺 Tidy up, what's on the floor (your things, kinds, trash, recycling, dishes, dirty clothes), one thing or one kind at a time, the spot glowing 📍 with others' spaces dimmed, drag or tap to put away, confetti, and the cleanup counts toward Rewards. (Pictures / Words tabs come with the phone word-list version in stage 7.)
5. **Deep clean** ✅ built: Clean → 🧽 Jobs. Picture cards (the bed, the dresser, the light switch) with a tool badge (hands, rag, spray, vacuum, trash bag). Jobs come from the room (tidy, vacuum, laundry if there's a hamper, make the bed and wipe the frame, dust each dresser/shelf/table, empty each trash can and the recycling, disinfect each light switch and doorknob). Each child sees only their jobs and the shared ones. Tapping a job shows it glowing on the map; ✔️ checks it off. Finishing a tidy checks off "tidy the floor". When all of today's jobs are done it counts one cleanup (once a day per person) with confetti. Parent settings (🔒 PIN): each job on/off, every day or every week, and who does it.
6. **Saving:** automatic save ✅, backup and restore ✅
7. **Phone layout and polish:** word list version first on phones, final touches
   - **Who the word list is for (decided):** parents first, then older kids who can read.
   - **What a parent sees first on a phone (decided):** today's progress, meaning each child's jobs and tidy-ups, checked or not. "Where does it go?" and settings are one tap away.
   - **One shared device (decided):** kids and parents use the same device, so progress needs no syncing or accounts. Everything stays saved on that device.
   - **📋 Today (decided, sample built):** a circle on the home screen anyone can open. 🖼️ Pictures and 🔤 Words tabs (phones start on Words). Each person's jobs, done or not, who did shared ones, and tidy-ups today. Settings still need the PIN.
   - **📍 Where does it go? (decided, built):** one tap from Today. A words list that starts from the thing, in the clothes / play / everyday / other groups: each kind picked for a space and each named thing, with every spot in words ("Dresser (Blue) · 2nd drawer from the top · Ava"). Tapping a spot shows it glowing on the furniture and the piece on the map.
   - **Tidy up Pictures / Words tabs ✅ built:** "What's on the floor?" has 🖼️ Pictures and 🔤 Words tabs. Words shows a checklist with a small picture beside each word, for kids who read. 🧽 Jobs has the same tabs, and the choice carries between the two pages.

## 10. Technical notes (for building)

- **App icon:** the title pink with a white spinning top tilted to one side (`icons/`). It's the browser-tab icon and the iPad Home Screen icon. The Home Screen icon opens the app in Safari, not as a separate full-screen app, because a separate app would get its own storage and the saved room would look missing.
- **Website:** GitHub Pages can serve the app from this repository, so it runs on its own site (no claude.ai viewer, and backup files download)

- Web app that works in any modern browser, and can be added to a tablet's home screen like a regular app
- Works without internet once loaded
- Everything is stored on the device, including photos
- Export and import use a single file
